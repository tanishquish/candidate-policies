Round Hill Association Hosts Debate for State Senate 36th District
By: GREENWICHFREEPRESS | October 8, 2026
Wednesday night’s Round Hill Association debate in back country Greenwich featured candidates for Connecticut’s 36th State Senate district, Jill Oberlander (D) and Joe Kelly (R).
“I think New York has the right approach. I think we should pause consideration on any data center,” Oberlander said. “I haven’t met anyone here who is interested in a data center.”
Mr. Kelly said building a data center would only create temporary construction jobs, since data centers employ few permanent workers.
Kelly said Connecticut had a “spending problem.”
“We have to take bold moves in order  to fix that by stimulating business. How do we do that? By lowering taxes,” Kelly said. “It sounds strange if you want to take care of unfunded liabilities, you ask for less from the taxpayer, but we have to make it comfortable for businesses to be here.”
“Jobs are leaving, we can’t have that. We have to stimulate the state by making it more attractive,” he added.
Kelly said he had lived for 8 years in a country with nationalized health care, but did not believe the US government should run health care.
“Healthcare should be a right, but I can’t have health care be run by the government because the government doesn’t run things well.”
Mr. Kelly said schools must be funded and Greenwich schools must be number one in the state.
“We do have to make sure we fund our schools. It’s primarily important for me, whatever is going to happen with federal funding, we have to have to make up for it with the state, because we can’t put our schools at risk,” Kelly said.
Asked what he would change about 8-30g and recent housing legislation, Kelly said the state should absolutely not have mandates on municipalities.
“I think 8-30g has created an adversarial relationship between the municipalities and Hartford,” he said. “I’m often concerned when Hartford is making decisions about what we can do within our town.”
Oberlander agreed local officials who know their towns should make these decisions on what kind of housing they need and where to put it, and the State should be a partner, providing data, resources, funding, help with infrastructure.
“In Hartford, I want to be the liaison between the municipalities and the State to ensure we’re getting the support we need and to push back and fight against overreach on the part of the state.”
“What I won’t do is give into pressure to support bills that aren’t good for our community. I will be an advocate for our district and our local zoning laws.”
Kelly had a short rebuttal.
“We’d vote it out,” he said of 8-30g. “Why it’s in place, I have no idea.”
